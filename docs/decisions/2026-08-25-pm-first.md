# Decision: v5 launches PM-first; accountability instruments go dormant

**Status:** accepted
**Decider:** Shane Skwarek
**Date:** August 25, 2026

## Context

v4 built Arcadia as an accountability instrument. Its governing rule — "Arcadia surfaces and attributes; humans decide and sign" — came from a specific failure: two weeks of founder absence, a dozen stalled projects, three leads who didn't act. The August 25 client-workspace scope document moved her unit of scope from project to client workspace and asked, in its §6.1, whether that rule survives the shift toward a project-manager/reference tool.

## Decision

Adoption first. The accountability aspect carries too much unfinished work to launch on; a platform that polices from day one will not get used, and a tool nobody uses enforces nothing. Arcadia launches as a single shared resource — a team-specific AI operating as project manager and project reference across client workspaces. Accountability instruments are introduced once the platform is proven to work smoothly and deliver quality output.

Concretely:

- **Dormant, not deleted.** The escalation ladder, Certification Ledger enforcement, and Dispatch stage enforcement stay in the codebase behind per-instrument config flags, default off.
- **Dormant means not accumulating.** No false-certification counters, pass-through flags, or escalation filings are computed or stored while the posture holds. Extending §5.7's own principle — covert scoring that surfaces later does more damage than the metric is worth — to the whole platform. When an instrument is enabled it launches announced, with counters starting at zero.
- **Detection is not dormant.** Stall signals, stale/missed/duplicate task correlation, and quiet-thread detection survive as PM features — the v5 goals require them. Only person-level attribution sleeps.
- **Safety controls are not accountability and do not sleep:** doctrine never auto-commits, nothing reaches a client without a named human, membership-scoped access, gatekeeper scoping, append-only audit.

## Consequences

- CLAUDE.md rewritten to v5: the single governing rule splits into a permanent rule (humans decide and sign) and a posture (inform, don't indict).
- The original failure mode — absence → unnoticed stalls — is knowingly unsolved while the posture holds. The bet is that visibility (reports, stale-task surfaces) improves behavior without naming; it is unproven.
- Retrofitting accountability later is socially harder than launching with it. Mitigations: the roadmap is stated openly to staff at v5 launch, and re-enablement is announced per instrument, never discovered.
- The re-enablement trigger (what proves adoption, and when) is open question #3 in CLAUDE.md §10, owned by Shane.
